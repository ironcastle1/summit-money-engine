# Source policy

Merlin separates sources into several practical classes.

## Primary sources

Examples include central banks, finance ministries, sanctions authorities, defence ministries, alliance organisations, port authorities and international institutions.

Primary sources receive high weight for their own assessments and announcements.

## Independent reporting

Wires and serious financial/international reporting are used for independent corroboration and discovery.

## Specialist reporting

Security, defence, maritime and regional specialist outlets can contribute when present in the curated source policy.

## State-controlled reporting

State-controlled sources are not discarded because they can reveal official narratives, claims and intended eventling. They receive a substantial independence discount and cannot, by themselves, generate high-confidence factual confirmation.

## Blocked content

Tabloid and outrage-driven domains are explicitly blocked. Unknown domains are not silently promoted to trusted status.

## Prediction markets

Polymarket data is treated as a market-sentiment input only. Probability is never used as evidence that an event occurred.

## Natural hazards

USGS data is collected, but routine earthquakes are filtered. A natural hazard must have sufficient magnitude/significance **and** priority industrial, logistics or security relevance to survive the assessment filter.
